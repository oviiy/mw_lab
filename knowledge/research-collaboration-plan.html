Candidate Research Collaborations:
Faculty Mapping and Project Designs

Working paper · August 2026 · link-only copy

Motivation and scope

This note maps concrete empirical projects to faculty whose agendas already create a natural opening for collaboration — not a wish list, but a set of problems where credit-market measurement is the missing half of the work.

For each person: what they work on, then three candidate projects. Each project is written so a reader can see immediately what is broken, why existing work does not fix it, and what would be built.

One rule runs through all of it: the contribution is the measurement half, never the theory half. Each project is chosen so the piece owned on the industry side is something the professor cannot easily do alone — usually construction of a credit or curve object that needs market conventions known from the inside. Where that is not true, the project is not worth either side's time. Second rule: public data only, evenings and weekends, nothing touching employer systems.

Background and comparative advantage

Four years at MSCI on credit derivatives pricing — CDS, indices, index options, tranches — including a bespoke tranche and significant-risk-transfer model taken from prototype to production. What matters here is hazard-rate bootstrapping, base-correlation mapping, and knowing when a quoted spread is information and when it's noise. Since March at Clearwater on fixed-income model validation: building calculations and defending them against Bloomberg across Treasuries, corporates, MBS and swaps.

Two other things. The 2023 MSCI note on what CDS implied about US default during the debt-ceiling standoff — implied default probability lower than 2011 despite far wider spreads, because the comparison turns on cheapest-to-deliver — was picked up by Reuters and the FT. And earlier, at Russell, multi-factor and style-rotation work on China A-shares, which is where the factor instinct comes from.

What I shouldn't pretend to bring: legal doctrine, econometric theory, Chinese institutional knowledge, or a publication record. I have production models and industry research, not journal articles. Converting the first into the second is the point, and that's the honest pitch.

Project 1.1

Trade-policy shocks and corporate credit spreads

Problem

Trade-policy events — Section 301 and 232 lists, exclusion grants and expirations, rate changes — are among the largest discrete shocks to real cash flows many issuers face. The open question is not whether equity moves, but whether credit moves, by how much, and for which firms.

We are trying to measure whether tariffs reprice the cost of debt, and whether creditors and equity holders disagree about what the shock means for solvency versus margins.

Current gap

Public debate treats “the cost of tariffs” as a macroeconomic talking point. Firm-level evidence on the financing channel is almost absent. Equity event studies exist; parallel credit studies with clean spread construction do not.

That matters because holders and creditors should react differently to a shock that compresses margins without necessarily threatening default. Without both sides measured the same way, the disagreement is invisible.

Proposed work

Build a timestamped event catalogue from the Federal Register and USTR with pre-specified coding rules. Map tariff lines to firm import intensity. Estimate abnormal spread changes over short windows, side by side with equity CARs. Then run the cross-section (import intensity, leverage, rating, supplier concentration), placebos, and matched controls.

Shu’s existing work already establishes differential real effects of trade-war shocks, so exposure mapping and identification are partly in place. The missing half is the credit panel: bootstrapping, curve construction, liquidity filtering, and knowing which quotes to discard.

Data Federal Register, TRACE, Compustat · 12 months · Risk thin single-name CDS for mid-caps — design bond-first, CDS as robustness
Project 1.2

Implicit state support and corporate bond pricing

Problem

Many state-linked issuers trade at spreads that look “too tight” for standalone fundamentals. The question is whether that wedge is a priced political guarantee — and whether it collapses when the guarantee is tested.

We are trying to turn political labels and government proximity into a measured credit factor: how much of the bond spread is support rather than credit quality.

Current gap

A large stock of state-linked credit is held passively through index rules the holder did not choose. If the spread embeds an implicit guarantee whose withdrawal is a discrete political event, those holders are short a contingent claim that has never been priced as such.

Decomposing spreads into expected loss, risk premium, and support is well-posed in credit engineering, but it has not been wired to Shu’s institutional measures of political proximity.

Proposed work

Use his political-label and government-proximity constructs (including the NSFC-funded work ending 2026) as the support signal. On the market side: spread decomposition, guarantee-event studies when support is tested, and aggregation to index level using published methodology only.

The label construct is his; the measurement of whether it prices in bonds is the open extension.

Data institutional access to ChinaBond or CSMAR; public index methodology; rating uplift notches · 12–15 months · Risk depends on data access outside personal control
Project 1.3

Firm-level political risk as a priced credit factor

Problem

Credit risk systems routinely use duration, spread, rating, and sector. They almost never use a clean measure of policy exposure. When a firm is heavily exposed to regulation, trade, or fiscal politics, that exposure is invisible in the risk stack until it shows up as a surprise loss.

We are trying to build a firm-level political-sensitivity measure that improves credit risk forecasts — and to test whether three independent measurement routes pick out the same firms.

Current gap

Equity models have rough political-risk proxies. Credit has essentially none. Without convergent validity — text-based, revenue-based, and event-beta measures agreeing or disagreeing for clear reasons — any new factor looks like another entry in the factor zoo.

Proposed work

Construct three scalable measures of political sensitivity: 10-K text, revenue dependence on policy-sensitive activities, and realised betas around political events. Build a credit return panel and evaluate out-of-sample risk improvement (not a return premium story).

Shu’s career defines what “politically sensitive” means as a firm attribute and where such measures go spurious — that is the defence against the obvious referee attack. The factor construction, panel, and evaluation sit on the measurement side.

Data EDGAR full text, TRACE, WRDS — essentially free · 10–14 months · Risk factor-zoo scepticism; lead with out-of-sample risk improvement

Co-authors Liangjun Su (Tsinghua), Joon Y. Park (Indiana), Robin Sickles (Rice).

Contact jhqian@sjtu.edu.cn · research page, with code for every paper

Qian is a structural-break econometrician. His central contribution is a family of shrinkage estimators — adaptive group fused Lasso — that find an unknown number of breaks at unknown dates in time series, panels, and panels with interactive fixed effects. Alongside it runs a functional-data thread he has already applied to yield curves.

His 2025 output shows the direction: refinement of the estimator, time-varying interactive fixed effects, and the dynamics of whole cross-sectional distributions rather than point estimates. He publishes code with every paper, which tells you how he works. An econometrician with a strong estimator and no compelling dataset should be a straightforward person to bring a dataset to.

Project 2.1

Structural breaks in the Treasury term structure

Problem

Market regimes on the Treasury curve are almost always dated by narrative after the fact — “post-QE,” “hiking cycle,” “dash for cash.” That is not the same as estimating when level, slope, and curvature dynamics actually changed, with uncertainty attached.

We are trying to date structural breaks in the daily Treasury curve as a functional object, and to test whether different curves (on-the-run vs off-the-run, nominal vs TIPS, cash vs swap) break on the same dates.

Current gap

If the off-the-run curve breaks before the on-the-run curve, or TIPS breaks when nominals do not, that separates liquidity from expectations in a way usual term-structure decompositions cannot. Without a formal break estimator on a desk-quality curve, those claims stay storytelling.

Proposed work

Treat the daily curve as a functional object (≈2015–present). Apply functional PCA and common-break methods — both already developed and coded by Qian — to level, slope, and curvature dynamics across multiple curve types.

The project lives or dies on curve construction: bootstrapping conventions, on-the-run selection, settlement and day-count, splicing across instruments. A curve no desk would accept produces break dates that are artifacts.

Data Treasury, FRED, NY Fed — free and official · 12 months · Risk breaks land where everyone expected; pre-commit to cross-curve disagreement as the headline
Project 2.2

Distributional dynamics of corporate credit spreads

Problem

Credit stress is monitored through averages — an index spread, an aggregate OAS. The economically interesting event is often a change in shape: the right tail of the cross-section detaches while the median stays calm, before defaults and downgrades arrive in a cluster.

We are trying to model the daily cross-section of corporate spreads as a time-evolving distribution, and to test whether tail dispersion leads the median into stress.

Current gap

There is no standard, formally estimated statistic for “distributional stress” in credit with inference attached. Methods exist for repeated cross-sections (Hu, Park & Qian 2025) and are essentially untested on financial markets that need a flagship application.

Proposed work

Build a large daily TRACE panel of corporate spreads, benchmarked to the Treasury curve (linked to 2.1), with liquidity and staleness filters. Estimate distributional dynamics: tail vs median timing, breaks in those dynamics, and lead–lag versus default clusters and downgrade waves.

The econometric framework is Qian’s; the contribution on this side is the panel itself — the unglamorous construction work that makes the methods paper economically meaningful.

Data TRACE, Treasury curves, published default statistics · 12–15 months · Risk real methods lift — dedicated reading time before a credible proposal
Project 2.3

Common structural breaks in dollar funding bases

Problem

Dollar arbitrage bases — CDS–bond, swap spreads, cross-currency, cash–futures — are the cleanest observables of dealer balance-sheet capacity. When they dislocate, the policy question is whether the system hit a common constraint or a set of instrument-specific frictions.

We are trying to test whether funding bases break on common dates, and whether breaks are in levels or in loadings — i.e. whether bases started responding differently to the same shocks.

Current gap

“Common vs idiosyncratic” is argued constantly with little formal evidence. Panel structural-break methods exist precisely for this question, but they have not been applied to a carefully constructed set of dollar bases.

Proposed work

Construct defensible base series (including CDS–bond with cheapest-to-deliver, curve mismatch, funding assumptions, and quote quality). Apply common-break and loading-break estimators to test joint vs separate dislocation, and whether breaks cluster on regulation or stress.

Data swap and cross-currency free; CDS index via university license only · 10–12 months · Risk data access; fallback is swap and cross-currency only

Project 3.1

Systemic risk with equity and credit-market nodes

Problem

Cross-market systemic-risk rankings are built almost entirely from equity indices. Yet the major transmission episodes of the last twenty years — 2008, 2011, March 2020 — ran through credit and funding as much as through stocks.

We are trying to re-estimate a ΔCoVaR-style system with credit nodes (CDX, iTraxx, major sovereign CDS, EM hard currency) and to test whether the ranking and lead–lag structure change when credit is included.

Current gap

Chen’s 2018 work ranks markets by systemic contribution using ADCC ΔCoVaR on equities. Whether that ranking survives when credit is added — and whether credit leads equity into stress — has no current answer. Without credit nodes, the map of “who infects whom” is incomplete by design.

Proposed work

Extend the existing estimator and framing with carefully constructed credit index series: roll conventions, continuity across index versions, spread-to-return transforms. Get those wrong and the results are artifacts. Report both the new ranking and the credit-leads-equity evidence as co-equal findings.

Data equity free; credit via published index spreads or liquid ETFs as proxy · 10–12 months · Risk reads as incremental — make credit-leads-equity the headline
Project 3.2

Capital-market effects of investment restrictions

Problem

Restriction events — delisting rules, outbound screening, entity listings, index exclusions — are adopted with strong claims about cost and effect. Almost none has been evaluated afterwards with joint equity and debt measurement.

We are trying to measure whether restriction events raise the cost of equity and debt for affected issuers, and whether the effect is permanent capital denial or a transitional price shock.

Current gap

The announcement-versus-implementation asymmetry is understudied. If most of the effect lands on announcement and partly reverses on implementation, that is evidence about whether the policy does what it claims. Market-opening studies exist; the reverse experiment is thinner.

Proposed work

Event study on restriction dates, debt-side measurement, and holdings aggregation for realised cost to holders. The main identification threat is simultaneous domestic regulatory action in the same windows — where domain judgment on institutions is essential.

Data EDGAR, holdings filings, Federal Register, CRSP, TRACE — all public · 12 months · Risk charged subject; pre-register, honest intervals, joint sign-off on interpretation
Project 3.3

Market pricing of fiscal and political risk

Problem

Fiscal and political uncertainty shows up across the sovereign complex: sovereign CDS, bill-curve kinks around projected x-dates, cash–futures and swap spreads, and the term structure of implied default probability. Commentary often quotes spreads without separating credit repricing from contract mechanics (delivery, denomination, cheapest-to-deliver).

We are trying to measure how much of an observed sovereign widening is credit repricing versus technical contract effects — and whether successive debt-limit episodes have changed enough to call it regime change.

Current gap

Sovereign CDS is thin, technically strange, and routinely misread. One public illustration already exists: implied default probability lower in 2023 than 2011 despite far wider spreads, once delivery is taken seriously. That remains a case note until adjustments are documented, applied consistently across episodes, and cross-checked against other instruments.

Proposed work

Build a multi-instrument measurement stack for four fiscal/political episodes: CDS auction and delivery mechanics, hazard-rate bootstrapping, Treasury curve construction, and parallel reads from bills, futures, and swaps. Rebuild independently from public sources only.

Data Treasury, FRED, CBO, CME, public DTCC; sovereign CDS quotes the main constraint · 12 months · Risk poor CDS liquidity — treat data quality as a result; employer boundary requires written clarity

Project 4.1

Market effects of critical-minerals export controls

Problem

Critical-minerals export controls — licensing regimes, quotas, entity-specific restrictions — are announced as if markets will price them immediately and correctly. Downstream firms that depend on the restricted input should show equity and credit reactions; the size of those reactions should depend on legal form (licensing vs prohibition, temporary vs indefinite).

We are trying to measure what a control announcement does to exposed firms, and whether reaction size scales with legal scope, stringency, and durability.

Current gap

Supply-chain policy is still largely built on qualitative vulnerability assessments. Finance work on the trade war often uses coarse annual dummies that discard the legal detail lawyers care about. The law-and-finance middle — correct legal coding plus market measurement — is empty.

Proposed work

Zhou codes the taxonomy: which measure is what, effective date, scope. The empirical half builds the event study, exposure mapping, and credit measurement. Upstream substitutes should react in the opposite direction — a built-in validity check. Track half-life across successive episodes as substitution and stockpiling evolve.

Data official gazettes, Federal Register, EU OJ, WTO notifications, EDGAR, CRSP, TRACE · 12–15 months · Risk contaminated windows from clustered geopolitics; flag clean windows as the headline
Project 4.2

A legally coded panel of trade-remedy actions

Problem

Trade-remedy dockets are public but unusable at scale: PDFs, inconsistent identifiers, no stable link to listed securities. Without a coded panel, both policy impact assessment and firm-level risk pricing are weaker than they need to be.

We are trying to turn the trade-remedy docket into a machine-readable, legally correct panel linked to firms — then test whether coded actions move equity and credit predictably.

Current gap

Correct legal coding (preliminary vs final, sunset, force date, product scope) requires trade-remedy expertise. No public dataset gets this right at scale. Infrastructure is slower than a single paper but more durable if others can reuse it.

Proposed work

Phase 1: schema and a fifty-case pilot with inter-coder reliability. Phase 2: full extraction and a versioned public release. Phase 3: market analysis and a firm-level exposure score. Legal coding sits with Zhou; pipeline, entity resolution, and market analysis sit on the quantitative side.

Data Commerce and ITC dockets, Federal Register, WTO, EU OJ · 15–18 months · Risk scope creep — hard-limit phase one to one jurisdiction
Project 4.3

Credit-market valuation of state-attributable support

Problem

Trade law must measure subsidies that often take the form of a promise rather than a cash transfer. Countervailing methodology needs a benchmark for that “benefit.” Credit markets continuously price contingent guarantees through spreads between state-linked and private peers.

We are trying to estimate the credit-market value of implicit state support and connect it to the legal concept of a benefit attributable to the state — then compare it with duties actually calculated in countervailing cases.

Current gap

Attribution doctrine and credit-market support pricing speak past each other. The legal concept lacks an operational market number; the market number lacks a legal definition that makes the comparison meaningful. That gap is exactly a law–finance problem.

Proposed work

Matched-pair estimation of support wedges, option-based cross-checks, and rating-agency uplift as an independent read. Validate on episodes where support was expected and did not arrive. Attribution framework from Zhou’s line; credit construction and estimation on the other side.

Data countervailing determinations, cross-market bond spreads, rating rationales · 14–18 months · Risk hardest of the twelve — matched-pair identification; second-year after 4.1

Comparative evaluation

New — how new the contribution is. Clear — five means few obstacles in the way. Maximum 30. Project number gives the professor: 1 Shu, 2 Qian, 3 Chen, 4 Zhou.

What the scores say

4.1 separates itself. It's the only project scoring five on edge, fit, data and novelty at once — his critical-minerals paper is recent and well cited, the legal coding is something only he can supply, and every input is public. Its single weak column is clean windows, which is a design problem rather than a blocker.

2.2 ranks second on strength, not on readiness. Highest novelty here, and the one where the benefit runs most clearly toward the professor, since it hands an untested framework its first serious application. It scores two on clear only because of the methods lift on my side — which is the one obstacle I can remove by myself, with two months of reading.

The 25-point tie is a choice about speed. 3.3 has the strongest personal claim, 3.1 is the fastest to a first paper and the easiest yes, 1.3 is the cheapest to run but the weakest on agenda fit. Take 3.1 to get moving, 3.3 to do the better work.

The bottom three are all clear-column problems. 1.2 and 4.3 score respectably on substance and fail on execution risk — access I don't control, and an identification problem that would take a year. 3.2 is the only one weak on edge, which by my own rule is the real disqualifier. All three are second-year work at best.

One thing the table can't show: three of these share infrastructure. Build the Treasury curve library once for 2.1 and it feeds 2.2's benchmarking and 3.3's bill-curve work; build the event-catalogue discipline once for 1.1 and it transfers to 4.1, 4.2 and 3.2. Running 3.3 → 2.1 → 2.2 means the second and third each start with a third of the work already done, which is worth more than a point or two of score.

Engagement strategy and constraints

Build the pilot before contacting anyone. One dataset, one figure, one page. For 3.3 that's a delivery-adjusted implied default probability series across the four episodes; for 4.1, twenty coded events and a reaction chart. Professors respond to demonstrated execution, not to proposals, and this inverts the usual cold-email dynamic.

On the message itself: one page and one figure, with the proposal as an attachment rather than the email. Lead with their most recent work, not their most cited — the difference is visible immediately. Make a small specific ask, twenty minutes or a reaction to one figure, not "would you like to collaborate". State the division of labour plainly: I build and estimate, I'm asking for judgment on identification and framing. On authorship, say only that it should follow real contribution in whatever order they think fair.

The principle underneath all of it: every project should be executable alone, on public data, if the professor declines. The collaborator is an accelerant, not a prerequisite. Built that way, four unanswered emails cost nothing but time.

Constraints

Public or personally licensed data only. No employer code, models, client data or internal documents — not as input, not as reference, not as a private sanity check; anything I've seen at work gets rederived from public sources or left out. Project 3.3 needs particular care since it revisits MSCI ground. Check the employment agreement on outside research and publication before starting, and resolve any ambiguity in writing. Everything lives in a separate repository on personal hardware.
