Discrete or Smooth Change?
Nominal–Real Treasury Curve Dynamics

Research collaboration proposal · Updated 30 August 2026

This proposal develops a methods-led study of structural change across nominal Treasury and TIPS term structures, with the August 2026 long-end episode used as motivation rather than identification. A completed public-data thesis establishes the baseline facts and exposes the central econometric problem: highly persistent, level-dominated curves can make capped mean-break screens resemble common regimes. The proposed collaboration asks whether common mean breaks, loading breaks, or smooth time-varying loadings are the appropriate representation—and whether any specification can reject the simpler explanation that both curves followed the policy-sensitive rate level.

Purpose and contribution

Research question and proposed contribution

In a highly persistent, level-dominated nominal–real panel, should change be represented as common factor-mean breaks, loading breaks, or smooth time variation—and which model can reject “both curves followed the policy rate”?

Most discussions date Treasury regimes narratively. The completed screen reveals the opposite danger as well: a flexible piecewise-mean model can mechanically redescribe persistent rate cycles as many discrete regimes. The proposed project therefore makes the discrete-versus-smooth representation itself the first empirical question.

Economic content

Common level movements are expected when nominal and real curves share policy-rate and real-rate shocks; they do not identify fiscal risk or market functioning. The informative objects are innovations, residual shape, loading stability, and cross-curve differences after removing the common level.

Methodological content

The project connects Professor Qian’s common-break estimators, functional yield-curve work, and smooth-change research. It offers an application in which persistence, curve construction, and model choice are inseparable.

Evidence-based claim and open question. The baseline level screens align, but changes and shape-only screens do not. The collaboration question is which formal representation survives dependence-preserving inference and can separate shared policy-rate exposure from genuine changes in cross-maturity transmission.

Core specifications

The proposed estimator begins from a common functional representation of nominal and real curves:

\[ y_{ct}(\tau)=\mu_c(\tau)+\boldsymbol{\lambda}_c(\tau,s_t)'\mathbf f_t+\varepsilon_{ct}(\tau),\qquad c\in\{N,R\}. \tag{P1} \]

The state \(s_t\) may be discrete, while \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_c(\tau,s_t)\) allows the transmission of common shocks across maturities to change.

A Qian–Su-style group fused-Lasso formulation estimates a sparse set of common coefficient changes rather than fixing the number of dates:

\[ \widehat{\mathbf B}=\arg\min_{\mathbf B}\left\{\frac{1}{NT}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl(y_{it}-\mathbf x_{it}'\boldsymbol\beta_{it}\bigr)^2+\lambda_T\sum_{t=2}^{T}\omega_t\left\lVert\boldsymbol\beta_t-\boldsymbol\beta_{t-1}\right\rVert_2\right\}. \tag{P2} \]

A selected nonzero group difference identifies a common boundary; adaptive weights \(\omega_t\) sharpen selection.

The competing smooth specification replaces discrete states with continuously evolving loadings:

\[ y_{ct}(\tau)=\mu_c(\tau)+\boldsymbol\lambda_c\!\left(\tau,\frac{t}{T}\right)'\mathbf f_t+u_{ct}(\tau), \qquad \boldsymbol\lambda_c(\tau,\cdot)\ \text{smooth}. \tag{P3} \]

Both models are evaluated after conditioning factor dynamics on the effective federal funds rate:

\[ f_{ctk}=\alpha_{ck}+\rho_{ck}f_{c,t-1,k}+\beta_{ck}r_t+\gamma_{ck}\Delta r_t+\delta_{ck}r_{t-1}+v_{ctk}. \tag{P4} \]

The central falsification test is whether any common mean or loading change remains in \(v_{ctk}\), rather than merely reappearing in persistent yield levels.

  1. Functional common breaks: re-estimate breaks inside a dependence-preserving bootstrap and report date confidence sets, rather than conditioning on screened dates.
  2. Loading breaks and PCA stability: compare full-sample, pre-event, rolling, and joint functional factors; test whether the same shocks begin moving different parts of the curve differently.
  3. Discrete versus smooth change: compare abrupt break specifications with the smooth time-varying framework developed in Professor Qian’s recent work.
  4. Cross-curve timing: construct formal confidence sets for lead–lag claims instead of matching point estimates informally.

Extensions requiring joint work

  • Direct plumbing measures: on/off-the-run spreads, bid-ask spreads, depth, repo specialness, dealer inventories, and security-level curve-fitting residuals.
  • Nominal–real breakevens, term-premium estimates, Treasury convenience-yield proxies, and issuance or duration-supply measures.
  • Daily versus weekly estimates and alternative maturity grids.
  • Simulation calibrated to the persistence and covariance of Treasury factor scores.
  • Pre-specified treatment of the Treasury’s 6 December 2021 curve-methodology change.
Main threat: an estimator may label persistent monetary-policy evolution as a sequence of discrete regimes. The discrete-versus-smooth comparison is therefore part of the economic question, not a robustness footnote.
