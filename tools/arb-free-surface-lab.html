Arbitrage-Free Surface Lab

An option price surface is a probability distribution in disguise. Drag the smile until the disguise slips: the risk-neutral density goes negative, and Dupire local volatility stops existing — in exactly the same place, because σ²loc = (∂w/∂T) / g(k). The numerator is the calendar condition, the denominator is the butterfly condition, and local volatility exists if and only if both hold.

What this tab is for: proving that an option surface and a probability distribution are the same object. Pick a maturity, drag a white knot on the smile, and watch three things move together — the smile turns red where it has become impossible, the density below it dips under zero, and the local-vol heatmap loses cells. They are the same failure seen three ways. Try this: load Already broken, look at the red band, then press Repair and watch every badge turn green and the report tell you how many basis points it had to move.

What this tab is for: the risk you actually carry, and one trap. Because the surface is held in moneyness, moving the underlying slides an option along the smile, so its vol changes even though the surface has not. That makes two different deltas for the same option, and the second panel shows the gap. On a skewed book the gap is not a rounding error — hedge on the wrong convention and your delta is wrong by the amount printed under that chart.

Variance swaps, forward variance and moments

Variance-swap strike is the log contract, 2·E[−ln(ST/F)], integrated over a wide grid. The premium over ATM vol is what skew and convexity are worth. A negative forward variance between two expiries is calendar arbitrage. E[S]/F and tail mass are truncation diagnostics: if they drift from 1 and 0, distrust the row.

Notation, colours and conventions
the fitted surface arbitrage: g(k) < 0, or a calendar crossing your quotes, bar = bid/ask extrapolated: nobody quoted here
k = ln(K/F)
Log-moneyness. 0 is at-the-money-forward; −0.10 is roughly 10% below the forward. Using the forward rather than spot is what removes rates and dividends from every formula on this page.
w(k) = σ²T
Total implied variance — implied vol squared, times time. The whole page works in this because both no-arbitrage conditions become simple in it.
g(k)
The butterfly quantity. Its sign is the sign of the risk-neutral density, so g < 0 means the surface implies negative probability. This is Breeden–Litzenberger (density ∝ ∂²C/∂K²) rewritten in the coordinates above.
∂w/∂T
The calendar quantity. Total variance must never fall as maturity rises, or you could buy the cheap long-dated option and sell the dear short-dated one.
σ²loc = (∂w/∂T)/g
Dupire local variance. Numerator is the calendar condition, denominator the butterfly one — so local volatility exists if and only if the surface is arbitrage-free. That single fraction is the point of this page.
RR (risk reversal)
Call vol minus put vol at the same delta. Negative for equities: crash protection is dearer than upside. It is the skew, in tradeable form.
BF (butterfly)
Average of the two wings minus at-the-money vol. The convexity of the smile — what you pay for tails in either direction.
Delta strike
A strike named by its option's delta rather than its price. "25-delta put" moves with the market, which is why desks quote in it.
Vanna, volga
How delta moves when vol moves, and how vega moves when vol moves. The two sensitivities that make wings expensive to hedge.
Variance swap vol
The fair volatility of a contract paying realised variance: 2·E[−ln(ST/F)], which depends on every strike. VIX is computed this way.
Lee's wing bound
Total variance may not grow faster than 2|k| in the wings. Break it and E[ST] becomes infinite — the surface implies an asset with no expected value.
SVI
A five-parameter smile shape. Cannot oscillate, and its wings are straight lines, which is why the repair fits one.

What this models, and what it does not

Forward measure throughout, so prices are undiscounted and rates never appear in a formula. Forwards are either given or recovered from put-call parity, never derived from spot and a dividend forecast. European exercise only, a single underlying, no discrete dividends.

Repair fits the closest arbitrage-free SVI slice under a vega-weighted vol norm, so it returns the nearest admissible member of that five-parameter family rather than the projection onto the full admissible set — and a different norm would choose a different slice. The wing bound is enforced on the fitted parameters as b(1+|ρ|) < 1.9, which is Lee's asymptotic condition, but the pass/fail badge still reads w/|k| at the grid edges rather than in the limit.

Tail-sensitive figures inherit the limits of the chain they came from. The log contract needs the whole strip, so where the quotes stop close to the money no variance swap is reported. Moments are integrated on a narrower window than the variance swap, because a fourth moment weights the density by k⁴ and would otherwise describe the extrapolation instead of the market. E[S]/F, missing tail mass, and how far the quotes reach in standard deviations are all printed per expiry so you can see which rows to distrust.
